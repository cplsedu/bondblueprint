The Introduction
You Are Not Alone In This
Why you feel the way you do, and why it makes complete sense
💜
Before You Read Anything Else
Take a breath. Drop your shoulders. Whatever is happening right now, the silence, the hot and cold, the feeling that you keep getting this wrong, none of it means you are broken. It means your nervous systems are doing exactly what they were built to do. This guide is going to show you why, and what to do instead.

When Marcus pulls away after a close weekend, your system fires an alarm. You reach. He retreats. You reach harder. He disappears for days. Neither of you is doing this on purpose. Both of you are stuck in a loop your nervous systems learned long before you met each other.

🧠
Here Is the Science
Your brain is wired to treat losing someone close the same way it treats physical danger. When they pull away or go cold, the alarm center in your brain fires. Brain scans show that rejection activates the same regions as physical pain. You are not being dramatic.
Eisenberger et al. (2003) · UCLA · Levine & Heller (2010), Attached

Why You Can't Stop Thinking About Him

Decode Him
What's Actually Going On Inside Him
His behavior isn't random. Here is what's driving it, in plain English.

When Marcus goes cold after a good weekend, his nervous system is not rejecting you. He learned early on that being fully known by someone meant getting hurt. So when things feel too close, too real, his body says: pull back before something bad happens. It is automatic. It is not about you.

Research by Stan Tatkin shows that people with avoidant patterns learned in childhood to self-soothe alone, because depending on others felt dangerous. Marcus does not pull away because he does not care. He pulls away because caring feels terrifying.

What he actually needs, even if he cannot ask for it, is a partner who stays calm and does not collapse when he needs space. When you can give him that steadiness, his system starts to relax. The cold spells get shorter. He comes back faster.

Stop These First
The Do Not React List
These feel like closeness to you. They feel like pressure to him. Every single time.
❤️
What Protest Behaviors Actually Are
Bowlby called them protest behaviors: automatic actions your attachment system takes when it senses your person slipping away. They are not character flaws. They are ancient survival wiring. The problem is that protest behaviors backfire with avoidant partners. They read emotional intensity as a threat and move further away, not closer.
The 5 Forms Protest Takes
  • 1
    Pursuit Escalation
    The double text. The "are we okay?" follow-up. Each one feels necessary. To someone who pulls back, they register as pressure, and pressure triggers more distance, not less.
    The rule: One message. Then you wait. The urgency you feel is a chemical state, not a deadline.
  • 2
    The Anger Protest
    Picking a fight about something small when the real issue is disconnection. The anger is real, but the target is usually a stand-in for "I need you to acknowledge I exist."
    The rule: Name what's actually happening. "I felt disconnected and it scared me" lands differently than a fight about plans.
  • 3
    Going Cold Yourself
    Disappearing to see if he reaches out. It feels like taking back control, but it's still protest. The problem is he often feels relief when the pressure lifts.
    The rule: Going cold to get a reaction is still the attachment system in protest mode. It rarely produces what you actually want.
  • 4
    The Ultimatum That Isn't Real
    "Maybe we should just end this." Said not because you mean it, but because you need him to say he doesn't want that. To someone who shuts down, a threat to leave is a reason to start preparing for exactly that.
    The rule: Only say things you mean. Fake ultimatums erode trust on both sides.
  • 5
    Erasing Yourself to Not Trigger Him
    You stop having needs. You agree to things that don't work. You make yourself smaller, hoping if you stop triggering the pull-away, he'll finally stay. This doesn't create security. It creates resentment.
    The rule: Shrinking yourself isn't the same as being secure. You cannot earn consistent love by making yourself easier to leave.

The Energy Behind Every Script
These are real messages you can send today. The goal isn't perfect words. It's the energy: secure, not desperate. Warm, not cold. Every script was built from your specific situation.
When He Suddenly Goes Cold After a Good Weekend
The Secure Script
“Hey. I notice you have gone a bit quiet. I am not going anywhere.”
Why this works: Removes the threat of abandonment without adding pressure. Say it once. Then stop.
📱 How This Plays Out
Goes quiet after a close weekend together
Hey. I notice you have gone a bit quiet. I am not going anywhere.
You said it once. Then you put the phone down.
When He Comes Back Warm After Days of Silence
The Secure Script
“I am glad you are here.”
Why this works: Short and warm. Welcomes reconnection without punishing the absence.
📱 How This Plays Out
Hey, how are you?
I am glad you are here.
Warm. Not a punishment. Not a debrief.
When You Want to Check In Without Pressure
The Secure Script
“Just wanted to say I was thinking about you. No need to reply right now.”
Why this works: Keeps the thread open without demanding a response. Low stakes. Means it.

When You Feel Desperate and Want to Spiral
What to Say to Yourself (Not Him)
“I feel anxious right now and I am going to take a walk before I send anything.”
Why this works: Regulation first. Response second. Breaks the loop before it starts.
