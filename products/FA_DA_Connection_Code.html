FA & DA Relationship Performance Improvement Plan

Relationship
PIP.

A science-backed, goal-oriented reset plan for couples where one or both partners have avoidant attachment , built for measurable change, not just insight.

This is not a document about who's wrong. It is a structured agreement between two people who want things to work and are willing to be accountable to that goal. Your attachment styles are not character flaws; they are learned protection strategies. This plan names the patterns, sets concrete goals, and tracks real progress. When conversations go sideways, this document is what you return to.

01
The Science
What Is Actually Happening in Your Brain
Understanding the mechanism before trying to fix it
🧠
Your attachment style is a survival strategy, not a personality flaw.

Attachment patterns form in childhood as the brain learns to answer one question: "How do I stay safe around people?" The neural pathways built then still fire today, automatically, before conscious thought kicks in. When your partner triggers that system, you're not overreacting. You're running an old program.

Bowlby (1969/1982) · Ainsworth et al. (1978) · Siegel, The Developing Mind (2012)
Fearful Avoidant (FA)

The Push-Pull

Negative model of self AND others
  • Desires closeness, yet closeness also triggers fear
  • Cycles between pursuit and withdrawal
  • Emotionally floods quickly under conflict
  • Fears both abandonment and engulfment
  • Often hypervigilant to relational threat cues
Dismissive Avoidant (DA)

The Fortress

Positive model of self, negative model of others
  • Shuts down emotionally under pressure
  • Withdraws to regulate, often without communicating
  • Highly self-reliant, uncomfortable with dependency
  • Minimises or dismisses partner's emotional needs
  • Appears calm but is internally deactivated
90sec
The neurochemical wave of an emotional trigger (adrenaline and cortisol) cycles through the body in roughly 90 seconds. If the feeling persists after that, it's because your thoughts are re-triggering the cycle. Jill Bolte Taylor, Ph.D., My Stroke of Insight (2008)
69%
Of recurring relationship conflict is "perpetual," rooted in fundamental personality and values differences. It doesn't get resolved. Healthy couples learn to manage it, not eliminate it. Gottman & Silver, The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work (1999)
⚡
Why the FA/DA pairing creates a self-reinforcing cycle

The FA partner's anxiety activates their pursuit behavior. That pursuit activates the DA partner's deactivation strategy, so they withdraw. The withdrawal triggers the FA's fear of abandonment, intensifying pursuit. The intensity triggers deeper DA shutdown. Both people end up feeling completely alone, for opposite reasons. This cycle is documented extensively in the Emotionally Focused Therapy literature.

Sue Johnson, Hold Me Tight (2008) · EFT research: Johnson et al. (1999)
Action: Do This Now

The 90-Second Body Check

  • Each partner: place one hand on your chest. Notice your heart rate.
  • Name one physical sensation in your body right now: not a story, just the sensation. Tight chest, shallow breath, knotted stomach.
  • Say it out loud to your partner. No context needed. Just the body sensation.
This engages the prefrontal cortex and begins to regulate the amygdala's threat response, which is the neurological prerequisite for productive conversation.

Action: Do This Now

The Trigger Swap

  • Each person writes their #1 trigger on a piece of paper. One sentence.
  • Swap them. Read your partner's without speaking for 10 seconds.
  • Then say: "I didn't fully understand how much that affected you. Thank you for telling me."
No follow-up, no defence, no problem-solving. This step is acknowledgement only.

03
Communication Framework
The 4-Step Connection Code
The only communication structure you need. Use it every time things get hard.
↳
Both partners commit to this structure. It removes blame and keeps the conversation goal-oriented rather than threat-oriented. It feels awkward at first. That passes. Screenshot this page.
1

SIGNAL: Ask First

Before raising something difficult, give your partner a heads up. Removes the ambush cue that sends DA partners to shutdown and hands them agency instead of threat.

"Hey, I want to talk about something. Is now okay, or can we find a moment in the next 24 hours?"
Gives DA a moment to prepare
Leading cold, when they're unready
2

SHARE: One Moment, One Feeling

One specific recent event, not a pattern or history. Pair it with one emotion. The more targeted the share, the less threat the DA partner registers, and the more likely they stay present.

"When you went quiet after dinner last night, I felt disconnected and unsure where we stood."
"When [event], I felt [emotion]"
"You always…" / "You never…"
3

REQUEST: Make It Concrete

Ask for something observable and measurable. If you can't tell whether it happened, it's a wish, not a request. Vague asks cannot be acted on and frustrate both partners.

"Next time you need space, could you text me saying you need an hour and you'll be back?"
Specific + measurable + actionable
"I just need you to care more."
4

RECEIVE: DA Partner, This Is Yours

Before responding, reflect back what you heard. The goal is not agreement; it's showing your partner their words landed. This addresses the attachment fear directly: "I am seen and heard."

"What I heard you say is [reflect]. I feel [honest response]. I can commit to [specific action]."
Reflect first, then respond
Defending before they feel heard
The Redirect: When It Goes Sideways

Either partner calls a redirect when: old grievances appear, blame enters, the topic has shifted twice, or neither can name the goal. It is not a shutdown; it is a return to purpose.

"I want to stay connected to what we were trying to resolve. Can we come back to [original issue]?"

Either partner can say this at any time. The other agrees to pause and return, even if it takes a short break first.

Action: Practice Round

Run It on a Low-Stakes Issue

  • FA partner: choose something minor and work through steps 1–3.
  • DA partner: respond using the step 4 script. It will feel awkward. Do it anyway.
  • Both say: "That was a good attempt." Growth is repetition, not perfection.

Emergency Protocol: When It All Goes Sideways
Call a Timeout

"I need 20 minutes. I'll come back and we'll finish this." Say it. Mean it. Actually come back.

Regulate First

Box breathing: inhale 4 counts → hold 4 → exhale 4 → hold 4. Repeat twice. Then return to the conversation.

The Repair Phrase

"That came out wrong. What I was actually trying to say was..." and that's enough. Clumsy repair still counts.

Reconnect Before Resolving

Physical proximity before problem-solving. Touch, sit close. Co-regulation precedes productive conversation.

Progress over perfection, every single time.
